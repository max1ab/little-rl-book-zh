The main idea

AlphaGo Zero [Silver et al. 2017] can be seen as a
form of policy iteration coupled with a model-based
approach. During training, it alternates between the

131

two phases:
• Improvement: instead of acting according
to the current policy, act according to an improved version of it. This improvement is done
through planning by searching in the environment and thus leverages the known dynamics
of the environment (hard-coded rules of Go).
• Evaluation: fit the current policy and value
function to the experience collected using the
improved policy.
It relies on neural networks to represent both the
policy πθ and the value function Vϕ .
A particularly striking aspect of AlphaGo Zero is that
training is done through self-play: the agent plays
against another version of itself. This means that no
human data is needed to train the agent, and that it
can learn from scratch by playing against itself.
