Blurring the line between

SFT and RL

In parallel, some methods propose to blur the line
between SFT and RL in order to leverage the strength
of both approaches. Indeed, one of the main advantages of RL is that it performs on-policy training, i.e.
it learns from situations that it actually encounters,
rather than from situations encountered by an expert

or a teacher. This is much more robust (as the student is likely to drift from the expert’s situations at
inference) and enables grounding [Carta et al. 2023].
But RL provides a sparse learning signal. On the
other hand, SFT provides a dense learning signal but
performs off-policy training.
On-Policy Distillation [Lu et al. 2025], which can
be viewed as a token-level version of DAgger [Ross
et al. 2011] applied in the LLM context, samples situations from a student model and uses a teacher model
to provide targets on these situations. It thus combines the advantages of on-policy training with a
dense learning signal.
