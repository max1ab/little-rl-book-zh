Common combinations

These objects each have a different nature and each
tries to predict a different aspect of the environment.
An RL algorithm may try to learn several of these

objects at the same time, and knowing which one
it learns is one of the first reflexes to have when
stumbling upon a new algorithm.
Rather than enumerating all combinations, we focus
on the patterns that appear most often in practice.
Value-based, model-free methods
These methods do not try to learn a model of the
environment. Instead, they learn a value function,
most often an action-value function Q(s, a), which
estimates how good it is to take a given action a in
a given state s. The policy is then derived implicitly
and on the fly by choosing the action with the highest
estimated value.
Deep Q-Network (DQN)

Policy-based, model-free methods
These methods also avoid learning an explicit model
of the environment, but unlike value-based methods,
they learn the policy directly. The goal is to directly
increase the probability of actions that led to higher
rewards in the past.
REINFORCE, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)

Actor-critic, model-free methods
Actor-critic methods combine both ideas. They learn
a policy, called the actor, and a value function, called
the critic. The actor is responsible for choosing actions, while the critic evaluates how good states or
actions are, and helps guide and stabilize the learning
of the actor. This combination is extremely common
in modern RL, because it often provides a good compromise between performance and stability.
Trust Region Policy Optimization (TRPO),
Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)

Model-based methods
Model-based methods learn, or sometimes are given,
a model of the environment. This model predicts how
the environment evolves after an action is taken. The
main advantage is that it allows planning: instead
of acting immediately, the agent can use the model
to imagine possible futures and choose actions more
carefully.
Probabilistic Ensembles with Trajectory Sampling (PETS), AlphaGo Zero
