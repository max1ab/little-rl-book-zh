Trust region methods

The previous policy gradient algorithms used the policy gradient theorem to get an estimator of ∇θ J(θ)
and then applied gradient ascent. Trust region methods take a different starting point: rather than differentiating J, we look at the difference J(θ) − J(θold )
between a candidate new policy and the current one,
and find that it admits a form that can be approx98

imated by a surrogate Lθold (θ) estimable from rollouts of πθold alone. The surrogate is only accurate
in a neighborhood of θold , hence the name “trust region”: the region around θold in which we trust our
surrogate.
This allows us to extract the most juice out of each
batch of trajectories, since we now have an explicit
objective we can optimize over several steps, as long
as we stay in the trust region. On the contrary, we
had to be more conservative with policy gradient
methods: nothing in theory justified taking multiple
gradient steps on the same batch, since the gradient
estimator is only valid for the policy that produced
the data.
